Why this model was selected

{{ metrics.selection_reason }}

MAE is the average error in dollars. RMSE weights large errors more heavily. R² is the share of price variance the model explains on unseen data — closer to 1.0 is better. MAPE is the average error as a percentage of the sale price.

Feature importance in detail

Two independent measures are reported, because they answer different questions. Impurity importance reflects how often a feature was used to split trees, which tends to favour high-cardinality features. Permutation importance measures the drop in R² on unseen data when a feature's values are shuffled, which is closer to predictive usefulness.

Interpretation caution. These are global association measures computed from the fitted model and the historical data. They describe which inputs the model relies on, not which factors cause a price. Feature importance should not be read as advice about which improvements would raise a home's value.

Limitations of these insights

  • All records come from a single mid-western US city between 2006 and 2010, so the relationships shown here do not transfer to other markets or to current price levels.
  • Median prices by neighborhood are simple descriptive summaries, not quality-adjusted indices; a neighborhood can look expensive simply because its homes are larger.
  • Some neighborhoods have very few recorded sales, so their medians are noisy.
  • Feature importance describes the fitted model, not the housing market, and carries no causal interpretation.
